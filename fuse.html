From fragmented capability to connected ecosystem.

A discovery engagement exploring the human layer around a live API Marketplace: how teams found, trusted, reused and contributed to shared capability.

The challenge

The marketplace existed. The human system around it did not.

Leadership suspected that discoverability, collaboration and reuse were breaking down, but there was no structured evidence showing where or why. The research therefore focused on behaviours, incentives, trust and ownership — not only interface friction.

My contribution

End-to-end research continuity.

01 · Protocol design

Research objectives, consent framing, scenario-based prompts and a separate executive track.

02 · Stakeholder interviews

16 interviews across architecture, SMEs, analysts, developers, UX, Heads of APIs and executives.

03 · Mapping & synthesis

Affinity clustering, cross-stakeholder pattern validation and a maturity read for leadership.

Research logic

Surface behaviour, not polished opinion.

The interview flow moved from context into current-state behaviour, then delivery friction, culture and future vision. Scenario-based prompts were used to make stakeholders describe what they would actually do, rather than only what they believed should happen.

Evidence chain

From raw conversation to strategic theme.

Observed pattern

Teams often found APIs through personal networks and tribal knowledge rather than through dependable systems.

Strategic implication

The problem was not only search. It was also trust, ownership and the way organisational knowledge was distributed.

Observed pattern

Delivery pressure and recognition favoured shipping something new over reusing or contributing to shared capability.

Strategic implication

Tooling alone would not fix reuse. Incentives and behavioural norms had to be treated as part of the product ecosystem.

The reframe
Move from a machine model to an organism.

The proposed direction shifted from fixed process and central control toward distributed ownership, adaptability and behaviours that reward reuse and contribution.
